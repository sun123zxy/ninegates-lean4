  • Ninegates.internalEmbedding

[Internal-coordinate embedding] For \(n\) ranks, the internal coordinate indexed by \(r\in \{ 0,\ldots ,n-3\} \) is the original coordinate \(r+1\). This gives an injective map from \(n-2\) auxiliary indices into the coordinates of the original hand.

  • Ninegates.mem_internalIndices

[Membership in the internal-coordinate set] A coordinate \(i\) belongs to the internal-coordinate set exactly when \(1\leq i\) and \(i+1{\lt}n\), in zero-based indexing.

Proof ▶

If \(i\) is in the image, then \(i=r+1\), so it is not the first coordinate and the bound on \(r\) shows it is not the last. Conversely, from \(1\leq i\) and \(i+1{\lt}n\), the auxiliary index \(i-1\) lies in \(\{ 0,\ldots ,n-3\} \) and maps back to \(i\).

  • Ninegates.internal_excess_le_six

[The total internal excess is at most six] Let \(c\) be a live all-rank wait at an admissible size \(n\). Then

\[ \sum _{\text{internal }i}(c_i-1)\leq 6. \]
Proof ▶

Interior positivity gives \(1\leq c_i\) on every internal coordinate, hence \((c_i-1)+1=c_i\) there. Therefore

\[ \sum _{\text{internal }i}(c_i-1)+(n-2) =\sum _{\text{internal }i}c_i. \]

The internal sum is bounded by the total tile count, which is \(n+4\) for a live all-rank wait. Subtracting the \(n-2\) internal coordinates gives the bound \(6\).

  • Ninegates.exception_card_le_internal_excess

[Exceptional internal coordinates are bounded by internal excess] Suppose every internal coordinate of \(c\) has multiplicity at least \(1\). Then the number of internal coordinates with \(c_i\ne 1\) is at most

\[ \sum _{\text{internal }i}(c_i-1). \]
Proof ▶

Let \(E\) be the internal coordinates where \(c_i\ne 1\). Each \(i\in E\) has \(c_i\geq 1\) and is not equal to \(1\), so \(c_i-1\geq 1\). Summing over \(E\) gives \(|E|\leq \sum _{i\in E}(c_i-1)\), and enlarging the finite set to all internal coordinates can only increase the nonnegative sum.

  • Ninegates.seven_le_exception_card_of_no_three

[Seven fixed blocks force seven exceptional coordinates] If \(24\leq n\) and there is no internal block of three consecutive coordinates all equal to \(1\), then at least seven internal coordinates satisfy \(c_i\ne 1\).

Proof ▶

Consider the seven disjoint internal blocks \([1,4),[4,7),\ldots ,[19,22)\) in zero-based coordinates. Each block is contained in the internal coordinates when \(24\leq n\). Since no block of length three is entirely made of \(1\)’s, choose one coordinate in each block where \(c_i\ne 1\). The blocks are disjoint, so these seven choices are distinct exceptional internal coordinates.

  • Ninegates.counterexample_size_bound

[Minimal counterexample size bound] Source: half-formal.qmd, Lemma #lem-bound. If there is a counterexample at some \(n\geq 6\) divisible by \(3\), then there is an \(n_0\in \mathcal S\) at which a counterexample exists.

Proof ▶

Choose the least size \(n\geq 6\), divisible by \(3\), at which a counterexample \(c\) exists. If \(n=6\), there is nothing to prove. Otherwise \(n\geq 9\).

Every internal coordinate lies between \(1\) and \(3\) by Theorem 74. There cannot be three consecutive internal coordinates equal to \(1\): deleting such a block would give a live all-rank wait of size \(n-3\). Minimality would force the shortened hand to be standard, and Theorem 87 would then force \(c\) itself to be standard, a contradiction.

Let \(E\) be the set of internal coordinates whose multiplicity is not \(1\), and put \(k=|E|\). Positivity and the upper bound \(3\) imply that every member of \(E\) contributes at least \(1\) to \(\sum _{i=2}^{n-1}(c_i-1)\). Hence

\[ k\leq \sum _{i=2}^{n-1}(c_i-1) =(n+4-c_1-c_n)-(n-2) =6-c_1-c_n\leq 6. \]

The \(k\) exceptional positions divide the remaining \(1\)’s into at most \(k+1\) blocks. Since no block has length \(3\), each has length at most \(2\), and therefore

\[ n-2\leq k+2(k+1)=3k+2\leq 20. \]

Thus \(n\leq 22\). Combining \(6\leq n\), \(3\mid n\), and \(n\leq 22\) yields \(n\in \{ 6,9,12,15,18,21\} \), as required.
